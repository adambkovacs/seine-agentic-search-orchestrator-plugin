Executive summary

A small, real, mostly grant-shaped lane

Three instrument families carry verified money into health-and-defence work in Europe, the company layer is thinner than the funding layer, and the clearest opening for a civilian network is a service role, not a portfolio bet.

The European Defence Fund's medical, CBRN, biotech and human-factors category has run for six years, peaking at EUR 57.8m in 2021 and settling at EUR 40m in 2026, EUR 222.7m cumulative. Only EUR 15m of that 2026 figure is openly competed; the rest goes to an incumbent consortium without a call [4]. DG HERA's 2026 work plan is roughly fourteen times larger at EUR 571m, but it is a civilian health-security budget, not a defence one, and Medifence, the one instrument built to bridge the two, still carries no disclosed envelope of its own [6].

NATO's DIANA ran its largest cohort yet in 2026, 150 companies from 3,680 submissions, with Human Resilience and Biotechnologies as one of ten challenge areas. The EUDIS Business Accelerator's published cohort roster returned 60 entries on 16 September 2026, against two earlier counts of 66 and 71 taken inside this same run, so the denominator is unstable and no percentage is published here [5]; only three to five of those companies are medically framed at all. The venture layer is close to empty: no health company appears among the leading early-2026 European defence-funding roundup's named deals, and the largest European mental-resilience round found in the whole sweep was EUR 130,000 [116].

Twenty-eight named companies carry an identifiable health-defence product across five sub-lanes, medical evacuation and logistics autonomy the largest at ten. Eight of the twenty-eight have an evidenced buyer relationship: five rest on a named armed force or government order, three on trade press alone (Avilus, The AO, Tencore). Ukraine's Brave1 runs no named medical track despite deployed battlefield hardware, and Germany's Avilus is best described as reported selected for a Bundeswehr evacuation-drone project, not as a concluded contract [112].

The white space is real but narrower than a first read suggests. NATO DIANA's 2027 Human Survivability challenge folds medical content, AI-assisted triage, routing and telemedicine, into a broader challenge rather than naming it, closed applications on 3 July 2026, and does accept a consortium application provided every member firm is eligible and one company leads it; only universities and non-profit partners remain excluded, not the network form itself. The strongest open door in this dataset is not a funding line but a service gap: Europe has no equivalent of the United States' Medical Technology Enterprise Consortium, an 800-plus-member body that has moved USD 1.6bn in federal funding through a single membership, contracting and matchmaking channel [10]. A civilian network can plausibly build the membership and matchmaking half of that role and the regulatory and clinical-evidence brokerage the Medical Device Regulation's Article 346 carve-out makes necessary; it cannot replicate MTEC's contracting authority, since no European Other Transaction Authority equivalent exists.

Funders & instruments n=11 · mean 0.81

The best-evidenced group in the corpus, mean composite 0.81. Eleven findings, almost all read directly from Commission and NATO primary documents.

F1 SOLID 0.86

The EDF's medical, CBRN, biotech and human-factors category has run at EUR 57.8m in 2021, EUR 24.9m in 2022, EUR 40m in 2023, EUR 25m in 2024, EUR 35m in 2025 and EUR 40m in 2026, totalling EUR 222.7m or 3.50 percent of the EUR 6,367.46m EDF envelope, with no 2027 allocation yet published. [4]

F2 SOLID 0.86

Of the EUR 40m in the EDF 2026 medical line, split across three topics at EUR 6.5m, EUR 18.5m and EUR 15m, only the EUR 15m CBRN decontamination topic is openly competed; the EUR 25m medical countermeasures alliance pair is awarded without a call to the incumbent framework-partnership consortium. [4]

F3 SOLID 0.92

DG HERA's 2026 work plan carries roughly EUR 571m, about EUR 424.5m from EU4Health and about EUR 146.5m from Horizon Europe, with EUR 292.5m of it in advanced medical countermeasure research and development. [6]

F4 SOLID 0.86

HERA Invest, a non-dilutive venture-debt instrument delivered through the European Investment Bank, receives a EUR 30m top-up in 2026 and three new loan agreements, on a published forecast of reaching EUR 200m by 2027. [6]

F5 SOLID 0.92

Medifence, committed in the EU Medical Countermeasures Strategy and taken up in HERA's 2026 work plan, is the only named instrument designed to bridge civilian health and defence, with five action strands spanning countermeasure shortlists, antitoxin discovery platforms, joint procurement and stockpiling, and access to wound care and medical devices in civilian and military settings. [6]

F6 SOLID 0.86

NATO DIANA's 2026 cohort is its largest: 150 companies from 24 Allied countries selected from a record 3,680 submissions, roughly 15 per challenge area, with access to 16 accelerator sites and 200-plus test centres, and Human Resilience and Biotechnologies as one of the ten challenge areas. [8]

F7 SHAKY 0.54

DIANA pays roughly EUR 100,000 in Phase 1 contractual funding over a six-month programme, with top performers eligible for up to EUR 300,000 more in Phase 2. [115]

DIANA's own 2026 announcement states no amount; the figure rests on a national delivery-partner page plus a NATO confirmation for the 2025 cohort, so treat it as the working rate rather than a published 2026 term.

F8 SOLID 0.86

The EUDIS Business Accelerator carries a EUR 6.2m indicative budget in 2026 and pays EUR 120,000 per company plus EUR 50,000 to the top three teams across two cohorts of at least 20; health sits inside its stated scope through technology theme 5, which names medical triage, casualty-tracking automation and wearable biosensors, but there is no ring-fenced medical budget line and no health-specialised programme. [7]

F9 SOLID 0.86

The European Innovation Council opened to defence and dual-use technologies on 17 June 2026 with grants to EUR 2.5m and equity to EUR 30m, and runs two distinct scale-up windows: the EUR 100m STEP Scale Up Defence, whose named sectors exclude health and biotech, and the general EUR 300m STEP Scale Up, whose three eligible areas include Biotechnologies. [21]

F10 SOFT 0.60

Neither the EDA's Hub for EU Defence Innovation, nor Ukraine's Brave1, nor the national defence-innovation hubs in Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Denmark and France publishes a medical funding line open to external companies. [26]

An absence-of-evidence claim assembled from programme pages; the EDA runs CBRN and Human Factors CapTechs and Germany's Cyber Innovation Hub ships military-medical digitalisation, so write it as no published medical funding line, never as these bodies not working on military medicine.

F11 SOFT 0.76

The health-defence funding map in Europe is bimodal: the civilian health side is large, openly competed and medically literate, while the defence side is small, partly pre-allocated and medically thin, and the only named bridge between them has no published budget line. [6]

Startups & GTM n=9 · mean 0.73

Nine findings on named companies and routes to market, mean composite 0.73. Programme facts here are strong; company-level claims are mostly SOFT because they rest on trade press rather than contracts.

F12 SOFT 0.74

iMEDCAP is the largest named health-defence industrial vehicle in Europe: an EDF 2022 research action worth EUR 24,885,861.25 at 100 percent EU funding over 36 months, coordinated by the Technical University of Munich, with 22 named entities across nine countries on the official factsheet. [9]

The factsheet is the 2023 award snapshot and the consortium's own 2026 press describes 24 partners; use 22 for any member list and attribute 24 as self-description. The factsheet's Philips GmbH Market DACH - France row is a primary-source error and should be flagged, not propagated.

F13 SOLID 0.92

iMEDCAP demonstrated a complete autonomous casualty chain at NATO's Vigorous Warrior 2026 exercise in Estonia on 12 June 2026, running drone search, unmanned ground extraction, in-transit robotic treatment under remote physician supervision, and aerial evacuation as one integrated sequence. [17]

F14 SOFT 0.56

Avilus flew its fully electric Grille X4 medevac multicopter on 2 June 2026 and was reported in July 2026 trade press as selected for the Bundeswehr's Uncrewed Qualified Airborne Patient Transport project, with Janes specifying 750 kg maximum take-off weight, up to 135 kg payload and 51 km range. [112]

No contract award is confirmed: the claim's own strongest source describes only ongoing evaluation, two of four original sources are unretrievable, and the contract-concluded-early-April-2026 date does not survive. Use selection language, not contract language.

F15 SOLID 0.92

Across the three published EUDIS Business Accelerator cohorts, only three companies are strictly medical and five have any health or human-performance angle. The denominator is unstable: a verification fetch returned 60 entries on 16 September 2026 against earlier counts of 66 and 71 inside the same run, so no percentage is published. [5]

F16 SOLID 0.80

Portal Biotech, a London single-molecule protein-sequencing company positioned for biosecurity and point-of-need diagnostics, raised a USD 35m Series A announced 30 June 2025 co-led by the NATO Innovation Fund and Earlybird. [16]

F17 SOLID 0.80

Finland's Ministry of Defence confirms two national selections into DIANA's 2026 Human Resilience and Biotechnologies challenge: Aboa Space Research, building a handheld AI-assisted fluorescence microscope for field assessment of CBRN risk, wound infection and radiation exposure, and SEATOM Technologies. [36]

F18 SOFT 0.68

Ukraine has industrialised robotic casualty evacuation at a scale no NATO member approaches, with unmanned ground vehicles running more than 100,000 logistics and evacuation missions in 2026 and operating units growing from 117 at the end of 2025 to 230 by mid-2026, supplied by vendors including ARX Robotics, Milrem, Roboneers and Tencore. [127]

Mission and unit counts come from one trade outlet reporting Ukrainian military figures and were not independently corroborated; the vendor attributions are separately sourced and firmer than the aggregate numbers.

F19 SOFT 0.70

Seven distinct routes to market are observable in this sector and none of them is a health procurement: the accelerator voucher as credential, EDF consortium membership, a national defence ministry contract after exercise validation, Ukrainian field trials as a de facto qualification lane, a joint venture for manufacturing scale, a health rather than a defence budget line, and a NATO medical exercise as the shop window. [9]

F20 SHAKY 0.48

The venture layer under health-defence in Europe is close to empty: the only European mental-resilience company found with an explicit forces go-to-market raised EUR 130,000 in pre-seed, and no health company appears among the eleven named deals in the leading early-2026 European defence and resilience funding roundup. [116]

Both halves are single-sourced and the funding-roundup half rests on one commercial league table; a second independent dataset such as Dealroom is needed before this is stated as a flat market fact.

Buyers & demand signals n=6 · mean 0.78

Six findings on who is actually asking for this capability, mean composite 0.78. Civilian health ministries carry real planning mandates; every verified purchase order in the dataset still comes from a defence ministry.

F21 SOFT 0.66

The operative buyer is frequently the civilian health ministry rather than the defence ministry: France instructed regional health agencies to have hospitals ready by March 2026 for 10,000 to 50,000 wounded, Germany's Surgeon General plans on roughly 1,000 wounded per day against about 15,000 beds, and the Netherlands is buying casualty trains and recruiting intensivists inside 14 civilian hospitals. [121]

The three national cases are separately sourced but reported secondhand from a leaked instruction and parliamentary statements, and Phase B weakened the general civilian-health-ministry-as-buyer framing; present them as three documented national cases, not as a rule.

F22 SOLID 0.92

NATO's Medical Action Plan, the central focus of the 65th COMEDS Plenary in June 2026, sets five priority areas: regulatory frameworks and legislation, workforce, mass casualty planning, patient evacuation, and medical logistics. [15]

F23 SOLID 0.86

Blood has been reclassified as a strategic capability and the NATO Blood Panel's March 2026 correspondence in The Lancet locates the bottleneck in civilian personnel, equipment and processing capacity rather than donor numbers, asking civilian blood systems to run surge planning, invest in freeze-dried plasma and extended-shelf-life products, and integrate blood logistics into national defence planning. [29]

F24 SOFT 0.76

Medical personnel shortage is the binding constraint across every national plan, named among the most acute shortage categories in EUROMIL's 2026 survey across military associations in 11 European countries, with every national plan drawing on the same finite pool of intensivists, anaesthetists, trauma surgeons and ICU nurses. [118]

F25 SOLID 0.80

Germany's Act to Modernize Military Service entered into force on 1 January 2026 and extends a mandatory medical examination to all men born in 2008 or later from 1 July 2027, creating a population-scale medical screening, fitness-classification and records throughput problem procured by military career centres rather than by a health system. [25]

F26 SOFT 0.70

Denmark's Military Mental Training programme, developed by the University of Copenhagen with the Danish Veteran Centre, becomes a fixed part of basic training in 2026, which puts the buyer in a training command with a dropout-reduction target rather than in a health service with a treatment pathway. [20]

White space n=6 · mean 0.73

Six findings on what is not yet built or funded, mean composite 0.73. Four of six are absence claims, which cap at 0.6 on the evidence dimension by design; read this group as a reasoned argument from what is published, not as proof of vacancy.

F27 SOLID 0.86

The EDF category is formally named Defence medical support, CBRN, biotech and human factors but funds no human-factors topic at all, in either 2025 or 2026; human performance, cognitive readiness and mental resilience are named in the label and funded nowhere inside it. [3]

F28 SOFT 0.76

Four of the five NATO Medical Action Plan priorities are organisational, regulatory and logistical rather than product-shaped, and no identified European programme accelerates regulatory pathway work, clinical workforce readiness or mass-casualty system design, because every accelerator selects on technology readiness level and on a product. [15]

F29 SOFT 0.66

Europe has no equivalent of the US Medical Technology Enterprise Consortium, which connects more than 800 members to armed-forces medical requirements through a single membership body, contracting vehicle and funding channel, with 384 projects awarded and USD 1.6bn in federal funding. [10]

The MTEC figures come from its own site and hold; the no-European-equivalent half is an absence claim that Phase B weakened, so frame it as no single body combining membership, contracting and funding rather than as a total void.

F30 SOFT 0.60

No DIANA accelerator site is health- or medicine-specialised, with the Estonian site the only one listing healthtech among its focus areas, and Europe's principal medtech and life-science clusters show no defence or dual-use programme in their 2026 priorities while EIT Health's 2026 call is civilian-only. [30]

Assembled from site and cluster pages rather than a systematic survey; the DIANA site enumeration covers 12 of 16 and must not be presented as a complete count.

F31 SOLID 0.92

Regulatory entry, not ethics or clearance, is the binding constraint for health dual-use: there is no blanket military carve-out from the Medical Device Regulation, and Article 346(1)(b) TFEU is a narrow case-by-case derogation the Court has held since Johnston must be applied strictly, so a battlefield medical device faces the full civilian conformity route and a defence procurement route in parallel. [18]

F32 SOFT 0.60

The clinical design requirement has flipped from the Golden Hour to prolonged casualty care of roughly 6 to 72 hours, which rewrites the specification for every trauma device and triage tool; products optimised for a one-hour handoff are being designed against the wrong clinical window. [124]

Rests on trade coverage of a NATO medical evacuation report rather than the report itself; re-source to the primary NATO or COE-MED document before publication.

Timing triggers (Q4 2026 - 2027) n=3 · mean 0.74

Three dated findings on the calendar ahead, mean composite 0.74. These set the open/closed table below.

F33 SOFT 0.60

Two European funding windows close within a month of each other: the EDF 2026 call on 29 September 2026 with evaluation results in the first half of 2027, and the EIC dual-use and STEP Defence Scale-Up windows on 28 October 2026 with results in early 2027; the EDF 2027 work programme is expected around December 2026 on the 2026 pattern. [119]

The 29 September date is corroborated secondary rather than primary, the EIC opening date is disputed between 30 June and 1 July, and the December 2026 EDF 2027 publication is inference from a single prior cycle.

F34 SOFT 0.76

NATO DIANA's 2027 cohort begins in January 2027 with applications already closed on 3 July 2026, universities and non-profit groups not yet eligible, and a maturity band of TRL 4-7 plus an explicit carve-out for TRL 8-plus civilian solutions; the next Vigorous Warrior is not until 2028, leaving 18 to 21 months with no equivalent European medical validation venue. [113]

Whether a consortium may apply under a lead company is an unreconciled contradiction between Phase B agents and is deliberately excluded here; the university and non-profit exclusion is the only eligibility point that survived both, and the word not-yet is load-bearing.

F35 SOLID 0.86

HERA clusters four dated openings at the end of 2026, namely the first medical countermeasure industrial roadmaps, the second HERA Industry Days, the launch of a European Diagnostics Hub, and a CBRN simulation exercise explicitly including civil-military cooperation alongside EU Integrated Resolve 2026 with NATO, ahead of the European Health Data Space milestone of 25 March 2027 when Health Data Access Bodies must exist. [6]

The HERA dates are primary; the EHDS dates come from an 18-month-old law-firm advisory and should be re-verified against Regulation (EU) 2025/327 before publication.

Red team

Adversarial review verdicts

A five-step adversarial pass (steelman, attack, negate, fail-scenario, correction) was run against the fourteen load-bearing conclusions of the research. Upheld means kept as framed, qualified or downgraded means kept only in a corrected or narrower form, rejected means the original phrasing was dropped and replaced. This is also where the report's live numbers were corrected: the EUDIS cohort denominator moves across 66, 71 and a verification count of 60 on same-day re-fetches, the DIANA 2027 window is confirmed closed 3 July 2026 with collaborative applications allowed under one lead company, the Avilus-Bundeswehr relationship is described as reported selected rather than contracted, and the Medical Technology Enterprise Consortium comparison is presented as a qualified service-role argument rather than a portfolio thesis.

C1 - health x defence in Europe is a real, funded lane upheld kept as originally framed

Three instrument families with primary-source-verified money, verified independently at Gate A and Gate B and re-read at the work-programme level by both Phase B agents. No attack lands. Lead with this and with the EUR 222.7m cumulative figure, which is the strongest single number in the corpus.

C2 - the lane is under-crowded qualified kept in a narrower, corrected form

The counts are real; the word is not. Publish the measured shares with denominators and a retrieval date and let the reader draw the conclusion. Drop 'under-crowded', which asserts an unexamined reason for the thinness, and state plainly that the thinness is measured from absence claims and that this run's absence claims had a 12-of-22 non-survival rate under testing.

C3 - the money is bimodal downgraded kept only in a reduced form

Bimodal in instrument type, not in addressable amount. The comparison as framed sets an agency budget against a topic budget against a voucher. Restate as: large civilian health-security envelopes that are neither defence-badged nor dual-use-earmarked, against small defence-badged instruments with explicit medical lines - and note that Medifence, the named civil-military instrument, carries no disclosed envelope in either of two primary documents, while EDF's EUR 40m is EUR 15m to a newcomer.

C4 - civilian health ministries are among the real buyers downgraded kept only in a reduced form

Split the claim in two, as the skeptic proposed and further evidence supports. Civilian health systems hold the mandate, the planning figures, the capacity and the largest unfunded requirement. Every verified purchase order in this dataset was issued by a defence ministry. The health budget is the least-contested grant door, not a procurement door. Do not use the word buyer for a health ministry anywhere in the report.

C5 - blood and plasma is a concrete sub-lane qualified kept in a narrower, corrected form

Publish the requirement, not the gap. The NATO Blood Panel demand signal is SOLID and dated; the supply gap that made it look investable collapsed, and no European freeze-dried plasma supplier list was ever assembled. Say that the named bottleneck is personnel, equipment and processing capacity in civilian blood establishments, which is an operations and logistics-software problem, and that the only surviving product-gap claim is the narrow 'no European synthetic-blood or HBOC startup identified in this sweep'.

C6 - casualty-care digitisation is a concrete sub-lane upheld kept as originally framed

The only sub-lane with two verified MoD contracts, a deployed product, a hard interoperability requirement and a dated governance vacuum. Publish at full strength, and publish the awkward half too: the largest award went to an incumbent civilian NHS EHR vendor, which tells the reader what the winner profile actually looks like.

C7 - medevac autonomy is a concrete sub-lane qualified kept in a narrower, corrected form

Separate validation from conversion, and name the competitive set. The technology is exercise-validated at Vigorous Warrior 2026 and Sabre Strike 2026; the single claimed procurement conversion is SOFT with its date struck and two of four sources unreachable after four attempts; PESCO UNATIS is indeterminate. Add what no phase said: the vendors here are robotics and UAV platform companies entering health, so a health network's regulatory and clinical-evidence advantage does not apply to this sub-lane.

C8 - readiness and mental resilience is a concrete sub-lane downgraded kept only in a reduced form

Policy-manufactured demand with no investable company layer identified and no EU funding topic. Denmark and Ukraine are meeting the requirement through public institutions; the entire European venture layer found is one company at EUR 130k; and the EDF category labelled 'human factors' funds no human-factors topic. Present this as a demand observation and a possible future market, explicitly not as a current sub-lane with a supply side.

C9 - the white space is the MTEC-style broker role downgraded kept only in a reduced form

Split the role and say which half is available. The broker function - membership, requirement translation, matchmaking - is addressable and is also the crowded half in Europe. The contracting instrument, which is what made MTEC's USD 1.6bn move, is not replicable by a civilian network and Europe has no Other Transaction Authority equivalent. Publish both cumulative totals (MTEC USD 1.6bn awarded; EDF EUR 222.7m across six years) without computing a rate, since MTEC's period is not disclosed in the retrieved source. And publish the MILMED COE counterexample honestly - seventeen years of convening without capitalising is the best available evidence about what a broker without an instrument achieves.

C10 - the white space includes MDR and clinical-evidence expertise upheld kept as originally framed

The strongest white-space claim in the report: two shards converging from independent legal instruments on a constraint the EU's own defence-simplification drive explicitly declined to relieve. Two publication conditions - drop the 989-refusals figure, which is single-sourced to a commercial consultancy blog and all-sector; and state that this is a service line rather than a portfolio thesis, because navigating a notified-body capacity shortage does not shorten it.

C11 - a civilian health innovation network can realistically enter qualified kept in a narrower, corrected form

Entry is possible, narrower and later than the thesis implies. Evidenced barrier: entity form (DIANA excludes universities and non-profits, and collaborative proposals require every member company to be eligible). Dated risk: the Regulation (EU) 2021/821 review, biotechnology explicitly in scope, consultation closed 15 October 2026 with evaluation running to 10 September 2028. Unassessed: clearance, on which the entire corpus is silent - publish that silence rather than reasoning about it. Pair this with a dated open/shut table of every named door, because most of them are shut at publication.

C12 - DIANA 2027 permits only single-entity applications (referee docket 14) rejected original framing dropped, replaced with the corrected finding

Rejected on primary-source re-fetch performed this phase. DIANA accepts collaborative proposals provided all member companies meet eligibility, with one designated lead company for communication, contracting and reporting. The skeptic was right to collapse it and the referee was right about the strategic consequence for the wrong reason - a non-profit network cannot apply as itself and its academic partners cannot join the consortium, because eligibility binds every member. Publish the combined form, preserve the words 'not yet', preserve the TRL 8+ civilian carve-out, and state that the 2027 window opened 01/06/2026 and closed 03/07/2026, a fact no prior phase reported.

C13 - grant-funded not venture-funded, framed as an opportunity rejected original framing dropped, replaced with the corrected finding

The opportunity framing is rejected and should be inverted. Keep the skeptic's restated observation - no health, medical or biotech company among Europe's largest disclosed defence-tech rounds of early 2026, and every company in this sweep funded at one to two orders of magnitude smaller cheques - and publish it as a named risk to the network's own model, since the model depends on portfolio companies raising follow-on capital that this market does not supply. Corroborate the negative with a second independent league table before stating it flatly, as Gate B already requires.

C14 - use 66 as the EUDIS denominator (referee docket 17 binding instruction) rejected original framing dropped, replaced with the corrected finding

Rejected on the same principle Gate B applied to the DIANA contradiction: prefer the later, re-fetched figure. The skeptic re-fetched the live cohort page at 15:40 and counted 71 (20+28+23); the referee's binding 66 (18+27+21) was set at 13:45 without the skeptic's input, and Gate B missed this second instance of the identical reconciliation failure it caught once. An independent verification pass then fetched the same page twice on 16 September 2026 and counted 60 both times. Publish no denominator and no percentage: three different counts inside one day is itself the finding, and raw counts with a retrieval date are the only defensible form.
